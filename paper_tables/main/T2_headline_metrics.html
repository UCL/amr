Table 2. Comparison of simulation outputs with estimates based on observed data

Simulation values are means over the 2022–2025 calibration window (1,461 simulated days), scaled to the global population using the run-specific population scale factor. Median (5th–95th percentile) across 7 accepted calibration runs.

Metric Simulation Estimate from observed data References
Annual infection deaths (millions per year) 9.6 (9.0–10.4) 9.5 Murray et al. 2022 (ref 1)
People on antibiotics on an average day (millions) 87.3 (87.1–87.9) 100.0 Klein et al. 2018 (ref 2)
Incidence of bacterial infection per year (%) 16.8 (16.7–16.8) 15.0 Vos et al. 2020 (ref 3)
Incident cases of sepsis per year (millions) 21.1 (19.7–23.1) 30.0 Rudd et al. 2020 (ref 4)
  1. Murray CJ et al. (2022). Global burden of bacterial antimicrobial resistance in 2019: a systematic analysis. Lancet 399:629–655.
  2. Klein EY et al. (2018). Global increase and geographic convergence in antibiotic consumption between 2000 and 2015. PNAS 115:E3463–E3470. The antibiotic headline target is set to 100 million daily users, not 130 million, because Klein reports DDD-based consumption rather than unique people on treatment. The revised target is a person-prevalence proxy that sits below the DDD-equivalent total after allowing for dose intensity, stock-sales mismatch, and wastage.
  3. Vos T et al. (2020). Global burden of 369 diseases and injuries in 204 countries and territories, 1990–2019. Lancet 396:1204–1222.
  4. Rudd KE et al. (2020). Global, regional, and national sepsis incidence and mortality, 1990–2017. Lancet 395:200–211. The sepsis headline target is set to 30 million, not 35 million, because Rudd reports all-cause sepsis whereas the model is bacteria-only; the revised target preserves a large bacterial burden without forcing the simulation up to an all-cause benchmark.

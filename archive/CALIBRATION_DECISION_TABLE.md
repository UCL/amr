# Calibration Decision Table

Use this after `2-3` successful `1m` `CalibrationMode::Full` runs with the same code state. The goal is to move from stable output patterns to small, causal parameter bundles rather than ad hoc retuning.

## Review Order

1. Overall and organism-level infection resistance fit.
2. Serious resistance hospital:community ratios.
3. Drug class mix and treatment selection behavior.
4. Microbiome resistance burden and persistence.
5. Downstream sanity checks: sepsis and mortality.

## Primary Calibration Rule

Do not lose the main objective: infection resistance fit is the top calibration target.

Use serious-R hospital:community structure as the next discriminator inside that primary objective, not as a replacement for it. In practice:

- Do not accept a change that improves H:C structure while clearly worsening overall infection resistance fit.
- Do not chase drug mix, sepsis, or mortality while infection resistance remains materially off.
- When forced to trade off in early rounds, preserve or improve infection resistance first, then improve serious-R H:C structure second.

## Run Acceptance Rule

Do not tune from a single run. Treat a deviation as actionable only if it is directionally similar across at least `2` large runs and preferably `3`.

## Close-Enough Rule For Policy Use

This model is for comparing policy options, not for perfectly reproducing every surveillance cell. That means `close enough` should be defined by whether the model preserves the right ranking and magnitude of policy-relevant effects, not by whether every calibration target is numerically tight.

Use three bands:

- `Green`: good enough for policy comparison without immediate retuning.
- `Amber`: usable, but only if the defect is acknowledged and not central to the policy question being tested.
- `Red`: not good enough for policy comparison on that slice; do not rely on policy deltas there yet.

### 1. Overall infection resistance fit

This is the primary gate.

- `Green`: mean infection-resistance error is stable across runs and small enough that the main high-burden organisms are directionally right, with no major systematic miss in the wrong direction.
- `Amber`: overall mean fit is moderate but the highest-burden organisms are still mostly directionally correct; the model may still be useful for broad policy comparison but not for fine-grained organism claims.
- `Red`: overall fit is being carried by low-burden organisms while one or more major policy-relevant organisms are materially wrong in level or direction.

Working operational rule:

- Treat weighted/high-burden organism fit as more important than the unweighted average.
- A single major organism with clearly wrong resistance behavior can keep this category out of `Green` even if the overall mean looks acceptable.

### 2. Serious resistance hospital:community structure

This is the secondary structural gate.

- `Green`: serious-R H:C ratios are mostly on the correct side of `1.0` where they should be, and the important hospital-heavy organisms are not grossly flattened or inverted.
- `Amber`: the direction is mostly right, but the magnitude is still clearly too weak or too strong for several organisms.
- `Red`: hospital/community separation is broadly flat, inverted for important organisms, or obviously implausible for nosocomial organisms.

Working operational rule:

- Direction matters first, then approximate magnitude.
- For policy use, an H:C ratio that is wrong by a little is much less serious than one that is flat when it should be strongly hospital-enriched.

### 3. Drug class mix

- `Green`: broad class shares are close enough that the model is applying realistic selection pressure by era.
- `Amber`: some classes are off, but the overall treatment ecology is still plausible.
- `Red`: the model is using obviously wrong drug eras or class weights, so policy comparisons would be driven by unrealistic prescribing.

### 4. Microbiome resistance burden and persistence

- `Green`: carriage resistance is plausible in level and moves in the same general direction as infection resistance for high-carriage organisms.
- `Amber`: carriage looks somewhat too sticky or too weak, but does not obviously dominate infection resistance in the wrong way.
- `Red`: microbiome resistance is so high or low that endogenous infection resistance is being generated by the wrong pathway.

### 5. Sepsis and mortality

- `Green`: broad scale and direction are plausible after upstream infection-resistance fit is acceptable.
- `Amber`: imperfect but still qualitatively usable for context.
- `Red`: only if they remain badly wrong after upstream calibration is reasonably stable.

Working operational rule:

- Do not block policy work on sepsis or mortality while upstream resistance fit is still the main limitation.

## Practical Stopping Rule

For a policy-comparison model, calibration is probably `close enough` when all of the following hold across `2-3` successful `1m` runs:

1. Overall infection resistance fit is at least `Amber`, and the major policy-relevant organisms are not materially wrong in direction.
2. Serious-R H:C structure is at least `Amber`, with no obvious flattening or inversion in the main hospital-heavy organisms.
3. Drug class mix is not obviously unrealistic for the eras being compared.
4. Microbiome resistance is not forcing infection resistance through an obviously wrong pathway.
5. Remaining defects are unlikely to reverse the ranking of policy options, even if they would still matter for point prediction.

## When Not Close Enough

Assume the model is not yet close enough for policy comparison if any of these remain true across repeated large runs:

- A major organism's infection resistance is consistently wrong in direction or grossly wrong in magnitude.
- Hospital-heavy organisms still have flat or inverted serious-R hospital:community structure.
- Policy-relevant drug classes are used in obviously unrealistic proportions.
- Microbiome resistance is dominating or underpowering infection resistance in a way that changes the mechanism of policy response.

## Policy-Relevance Test

Before declaring calibration good enough, ask one practical question:

`If two policies differ mainly through antibiotic exposure, hospital transmission pressure, or resistant carriage, would the current model respond through the right causal channels?`

If the answer is `yes, approximately`, the model may already be fit for comparative policy work even if several calibration cells are still imperfect. If the answer is `no`, the model is not close enough yet.

## Parameter Families By Use

### 1. Hospital vs community coupling

Use these first when the problem is mainly a hospital/community ratio problem.

- Per-bacterium `hospital_microbiome_r_multiplier`
- Per-bacterium `community_resistance_dilution_factor`
- Per-bacterium `hospital_resistance_concentration_factor`
- `hospital_profile_cache_retention`
- Per-bacterium `community_mechanism_reversion_multiplier`

Current live defaults worth remembering:

- Global `community_resistance_dilution_factor = 0.30`
- Global `hospital_profile_cache_retention = 0.999`
- Most `hospital_resistance_concentration_factor` values are currently neutralized at `1.0`

Interpretation:

- Raise `hospital_microbiome_r_multiplier` when resistant hospital carriage is too weak.
- Lower `community_resistance_dilution_factor` when community acquisitions are too resistant.
- Raise `hospital_resistance_concentration_factor` only when you specifically need stronger hospital weighting without changing community ecology.
- Raise `community_mechanism_reversion_multiplier` when resistant mechanisms should wash out faster in community carriage.

### 2. Microbiome persistence and transfer

Use these when resistant carriage and endogenous infection are moving together.

- `carrier_resistance_inheritance_probability`
- `infection_from_microbiome_dampening`
- `microbiome_resistance_multiplier_on_acquisition`
- Per-bacterium `microbiome_clearance_probability_per_day`
- `carriage_duration_log_odds_coefficient`
- `antibiotic_clearance_log_odds_per_unit_activity`

Current live defaults worth remembering:

- `carrier_resistance_inheritance_probability = 0.50`
- `infection_from_microbiome_dampening = 0.70`
- `microbiome_resistance_multiplier_on_acquisition = 0.50`
- `carriage_duration_log_odds_coefficient = -0.01`
- `antibiotic_clearance_log_odds_per_unit_activity = 0.50`

Interpretation:

- Raise inheritance or dampening when resistant carriage exists but is not feeding resistant infections enough.
- Raise clearance probability when resistant microbiome prevalence is too sticky.
- Lower clearance probability only when multiple `1m` runs agree that carriage-mediated resistance is systematically too low.

### 3. De novo emergence under treatment

Use these when the main issue is the rate at which treatment creates resistant infections.

- `infection_de_novo_multiplier`
- `microbiome_de_novo_multiplier`
- `resistance_development_inhibition_single_drug`
- `resistance_development_inhibition_partial_cross`
- Mechanism-specific emergence and reversion values where the problem is marker-drug specific

Interpretation:

- Change these only after deciding the mismatch is not mainly a reservoir / carriage problem.
- Prefer organism- or mechanism-local changes over global changes when the defect is narrow.

### 4. Drug mix and selection pressure

Use these after upstream resistance structure is directionally stable.

- Drug choice / scoring parameters
- Availability timing if a historical phase shift is clearly wrong
- Regimen pressure parameters affecting mono- vs multi-drug exposure

Interpretation:

- Drug mix can explain resistance mistakes, but tuning it too early can hide whether the ecology itself is wrong.

## Recommended Tuning Workflow

1. Compare `2-3` successful `1m` summaries and identify only the deviations that stay in the same direction.
2. Start with overall and organism-level infection resistance fit; treat this as the gate before secondary tuning.
3. Within the remaining stable misfit, classify each deviation as mainly one of: H:C coupling, microbiome bridge, de novo emergence, or drug mix.
4. Propose one small parameter bundle per mechanism family.
5. Run the next large calibration pass.
6. Re-check that infection resistance improved or at least did not materially regress before giving credit for secondary improvements.
